Letters by Email

Grandma doesn’t need the app.

She can receive and reply by email. No tablet, login, or special app download required.

Step 01

Your child writes on their desk.

They choose stationery, draw with digital ink, add stamps or stickers, and drop the letter through their desk slot.

Step 02

It arrives in standard email.

Grandma opens a high-resolution visual letter directly in her regular inbox. No new apps, logins, or tech hurdles.

Step 03

She simply hits Reply.

She replies just like any normal email. Her words arrive formatted in crisp typewriter ink directly in your child’s mailbox.

Design Philosophy

Made to feel like letters.

One letter at a time. No feeds, read receipts, or pressure to reply. Every detail is crafted to leave room for the person on the other side.

Hyper-Reactive Messaging

  • Instant reply pressure: Typing dots, read receipts, and online status demand immediate answers.
  • Fragmented chat bursts: Quick 2-word texts and rapid fire messages leave no room for complete thoughts.
  • Intrusive pings: Notification sounds constantly pull focus away from schoolwork, creative focus, and family life.
  • Hyper-reactive forwarding: Chain forwards, mass shares, and selective group forwarding breed exclusion and peer pressure.
  • Disposable streams: Words scroll by in infinite feeds with no sense of physical permanence or keepsakes.
  • Public discovery & feeds: Open search and algorithms expose kids to unapproved contacts.

Engineered for algorithmic urgency and constant scrolling.

Postmello’s Quiet Desk

  • The Postal Ritual: Deliberate delivery pacing slows down communication, leaving space to think, compose, and look forward to replies.
  • Room to express ideas: Multi-page letters written by finger, stylus, or typewriter encourage thoughtful storytelling and drawings.
  • All-inclusive forwarding & reply: Replies go either to the sender or to everyone on the letter — no cliquey side-threads or exclusionary partial forwards.
  • Reply-on-top tradition: Respond directly on a received letter’s locked ink, annotate in margins, or add new stationery sheets.
  • Quiet ambient arrival: New mail quietly raises the brass mailbox flag on the wooden desk with zero disruptive audio dings.
  • Built to be put down: Once an envelope slides into the brass slot, the correspondence is complete with no endless feeds.

Designed for patience, literacy, creative expression, and lasting keepsakes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers for Families

Everything parents, educators, and grandparents need to know about the Postmello experience.

How does Postmello postpone texting for kids?
Texting wasn’t made for kids. It’s built around quick replies, group chats, photos, links, and notifications. Postmello gives kids a place of their own to write, draw, be silly or thoughtful, and stay in touch independently with people you approve — one letter at a time.
How is unapproved contact prevented?
Unapproved mail is impossible by construction. Postmello has no public user search, no usernames, and no algorithmic recommendations. Connection requires a parent to open the PIN-protected Parent Portal behind the Brass Key and exchange a private 6-character Connect Code with another trusted parent.
How do grandparents without iPads receive and send letters?
Parents can create approved Email Contacts for relatives. When a child sends a letter, it arrives in the relative’s email as a high-resolution image. The relative simply hits Reply from their computer or phone. Replies return as typewritten letters — no ads, links, or photos can ride along.
How are past letters stored in friend drawers?
All letters are safely preserved in the cloud so they stay yours forever. When browsing a friend’s desk drawer, recent letters are cached locally for fast offline reading, while your entire history of past correspondence is pageable from the cloud.
Can siblings share one family iPad?
Yes! One family account supports multiple individual desks. Each child gets their own personalized desk style, engraved nameplate, private mailbox, sticker drawer, and friend compartments. Siblings can even write between desks at home.
Is my child’s data and artwork kept private?
Absolutely. No ads. No selling data. Your letters stay yours. Postmello is member-supported, so it can stay private, mellow, and built for the people using it — not advertisers.

Have a question about family setups or school use?

Write to the creators directly at hello@postmello.com. We reply to every letter.
